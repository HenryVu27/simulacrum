# Aporia Atlas

Each table lists every minimal way to restore consistency (a coherent position), who holds it, and the claims nobody gives up.

## Mind and body (Kim's exclusion problem)

| claim | statement |
|---|---|
| D | Mental states are not identical to physical states. |
| M | Mental states cause physical events. |
| C | Every physical event has a sufficient physical cause. |
| X | Events are not systematically caused twice over. |

| give up | position | held by |
|---|---|---|
| C | interactionist dualism | Descartes, Popper and Eccles |
| D | identity theory | Place, Smart, Lewis |
| M | epiphenomenalism | Huxley, Jackson (1982) |
| X | accepted overdetermination | Bennett (2003), some nonreductive physicalists |

Non-minimal positions (give up more than needed): eliminativism.

## Object permanence and access

| claim | statement |
|---|---|
| A | We know only what appears to us. |
| R | Knowing that something exists while unperceived requires access to it while unperceived. |
| K | We know that objects persist when unperceived. |

| give up | position | held by |
|---|---|---|
| A | direct realism | Reid, Austin |
| K | Humean fiction | Hume: continued existence is a fiction of the imagination |
| R | transcendental idealism, inference to the best explanation | Kant: permanence is a condition of experience, known a priori; Russell, structural realists |

umwelt bears on R: object_hidden: a learner that only ever predicts what it senses can come to encode hidden objects, and the impermanent world shows how much of that is forced by the world (H1, H6, H7).

umwelt bears on K: permanence surprise (voe.permanence) is the behavioural sign of expecting persistence.

## Hume's problem of causation

| claim | statement |
|---|---|
| E | All knowledge of matters of fact comes from experience. |
| J | Experience shows only constant conjunction, never necessary connection. |
| N | Causation involves necessary connection. |
| Q | We have knowledge of causal relations. |

| give up | position | held by |
|---|---|---|
| E | causality a priori | Kant |
| J | interventionism and direct perception | Anscombe, Woodward, Pearl |
| N | regularity theory | Hume (one reading), Lewis |
| Q | causal skepticism | Hume (skeptical reading) |

umwelt bears on J: passive versus active minds and the cause probe: does acting on the world give more than conjunction (H3)? The nonlocal world keeps constant conjunction but removes contact.

umwelt bears on N: the nonlocal world: consistent action at a distance, a regularity without a mechanism.
